# D-21 Curve-preserving geometry with a measured, reported tolerance

**Decided by**: architecture review (capability design closure, all four lenses, change 007) ·
**Date**: capability design, pre-public history · **Cited by**: R-08, D-14, D-22, change 011

Status: accepted, except the glyph-outline platform class (Determinism), which stays conditional
on the owner (inbox) and gates only the text-outline increment of change 011. Built by change 011
(slice S04 of change 013); nothing here is built yet.

## Decision

Clipper2 (D-14) stays the single topology engine **for booleans only**. A Core path-geometry layer
is built around it. The contract below is binding; algorithm numerics are implementation notes,
verified at implementation review against the oracles.

### Model, writer and output

- `VectorPath`: contours of line and cubic segments with a closed flag. Quadratics are
  degree-elevated exactly. Arcs, ellipse nodes and rounded corners become cubics without libm
  (IEEE `+ − × ÷ sqrt` only); the fixed "at most 4 cubics" rule is retired. Cubics split at cusps
  and internal tangent reversals.
- The strict parser keeps today's `d` grammar and budgets; the canonical writer emits absolute
  `M/L/C/Z` under the existing coordinate formatting, no implicit commands, closing as `Z`.
- **`DeterministicTrig`**: sin and cos in degrees with exact reduction and fixed polynomial kernels
  (≤ 2 ulp, exact at multiples of 90°). It replaces `Math.Sin/Cos` in bounding-box rotation, so
  world transforms, operand mapping and write-back are libm-free (declared: last-ulp bounds changes
  under non-quarter rotations).
- **Exact curve bounds** (derivative roots), also adopted by the layout bounds calculator
  (declared: align, distribute, fit and sub-node crop move by at most the old flattening error).
- **Adaptive canonical flattening** with segment endpoints always emitted and no silent clamp;
  point counts are computed in closed form and checked against the per-command point budget before
  allocation (`RESOURCE_LIMIT`).
- **Output modes** `curves` (new default) and `polygon` (the reference polygon as `M/L/Z`; bytes
  and counts change, declared). No fixed-step flattener stays.

### Tolerance: measured and reported, not certified

- Tolerance `T`: default 0.01, range [0.008, 100], evaluation-space units.
- The engine builds a reference polygon `P*` within `referenceTolerance`, the sum of planned
  shares: grid rounding `G = L·0.0005·√2` (`L` grid roundings on the deepest lineage, at most 6;
  2 for a direct boolean), the coincidence radius `q_c = 0.003` when a match applied, and arc,
  flattening, join and round-piece shares.
- **Feasibility**: `referenceTolerance ≤ T − m_min` with `m_min = 0.002` reserved for the measured
  part. An infeasible plan fails before the first Clipper call with `PATH_TOLERANCE_UNMET` and
  `details {tolerance, minFeasibleTolerance}`, doing no work and writing nothing. The default 0.01
  is feasible for every chain the stage model allows (the worst matched `L = 6` chain bounds the
  other shares to ≤ 0.00076); at 0.008 deeper matched chains fail fast.
- **Measured part**: the verifier parses the final emitted bytes. Reused and matched runs are
  compared with their exact source sub-curve by control-point distance. Fitted and polyline runs
  are compared two-sided with their `P*` sub-polyline by deterministic sampling at spacing
  `h ≤ T/32` plus a stated margin `h/2`.
- **Report**: `maxDeviation = referenceTolerance + measuredDeviation ≤ T`. A fitted run over its
  share falls back to its polyline; if that also fails, `PATH_TOLERANCE_UNMET`
  `details {tolerance, achieved}`, never a silent miss. `maxDeviation` is a measured estimate, not
  a proven bound, and docs call it "measured".
- **Topology events** (reported with positions, never charged): dropped contours, filled holes and
  unrefined intersections, in `topologyEvents[] {kind, at}` (≤ 256, then `truncated: true`).

### Sliver prevention (normative)

1. **Coincidence matching** before any Clipper stage, on exact input segments of fill areas
   (never on constructed stroke or offset pieces): two segments match on a common parameter
   sub-range when every control point of the overlap lies within `q_c` of the other segment's exact
   split. The matched piece reuses the other operand's vertices, so Clipper sees exactly coincident
   edges; no remainder with chord ≤ `q_c` is split off; one pass with deterministic tie-breaks.
2. **Intersection refinement** onto both exact segments, self-intersections included. Accepted
   when the residual is ≤ 1e-9·(1 + max |coord|), the refined parameters lie inside the producing
   flattening edges' spans widened by one edge, and sin θ ≥ 0.05 at the crossing. Otherwise the
   vertex is reported as an `unrefinedIntersection`.
3. **Exact source-segment reuse**: runs that follow one input segment are re-emitted as that
   segment (whole segments unchanged, partial runs as exact sub-curves ending at refined vertices).
4. **Width-based cleanup** (default `drop`, opt-out `none`): components and holes whose inward
   offset by `T/2` is empty are dropped or filled and reported; no retained contour is edited.

Scope: exact coincidence is guaranteed for AGD-stored results reused through stored-node operands
(parent and child, or siblings of one parent segment) through relative maps with largest singular
value ≤ 1. It is not claimed for independently fitted copies or runs ending at unrefined
intersections; their residue narrower than T is cleaned and reported, and a magnifying stored
operand carries the warning `PATH_OPERAND_MAGNIFIED`.

### Stroker and offsets (libm-free; `ClipperOffset` is no longer used)

- **Stroke region** = the SVG stroke region of the exact centerline: joins at tangent
  discontinuities (miter if the ratio is within `miterLimit`, else bevel; bevel; round) and caps
  `butt|square|round`. Strokes are built in the stroked node's own space, then mapped. The 180°
  cusp rule follows the measured renderer behaviour.
- **Offsets** over a canonical area A: `offset(A, +d) = A ∪ stroke(∂A, 2d)`,
  `offset(A, −d) = A − stroke(∂A, 2|d|)`, round sectors at smooth vertices and the declared join at
  corners, corners identified only from exact-segment provenance.
- **Constructed input** (a stroke or painted operand) supports round joins only; miter or bevel is
  `DERIV_UNSUPPORTED` with the two-step recipe (derive the stroke, then offset its fill), each step
  measured against its own ideal.
- **Joins** `miter|round|bevel`; the `square` join is removed (declared). **One miter-limit rule
  everywhere**: default 4, range [1, 1000] (declared cutover from `path offset`'s 2.0 and
  (0, 1000]); out-of-range arguments are `INVALID_PROPERTY`; a source stroke limit outside the
  range is refused, never clamped.
- **`path offset` end types**: `polygon` (default) is the area offset; `butt|round|square` strokes
  each contour with width `2|d|` (closed contours join at their start; sign-independent; reports
  `offset` and `width`); `joined` is removed with `INVALID_END` and a `path stroke` hint.
- Dashed source strokes are refused (`PATH_OPERAND_INVALID`); dash outlining is not built.

### Clipper stage model

- A canonical area has non-overlapping, consistently oriented contours. A fill area is the operand
  under its own fill rule; a boolean has no fill rule of its own.
- Stages, one Clipper call each: `N` normalizes a fill, `S` unites stroke pieces, `P` unites fill
  with stroke, `O` offsets, and one final `B` performs the boolean (difference and intersect take
  the first operand against the union of the rest; n-ary xor is one even-odd call). Two raw
  same-rule fills may enter `B` directly.
- Per lineage at most two area stages, then at most one `O`, and one `B`: `clipperStages ≤ 4`,
  asserted before the first call; `clipperStages` and `clipperCalls` are reported.

### Bounds (normative)

- Before the first Clipper call (int64 domain, scale 1000) every evaluation-space input coordinate
  satisfies `|coord| + E ≤ 1e9`, with extent `E = (w/2)·max(√2, miterLimit)` for a miter stroke,
  `(w/2)·√2` otherwise, the same with `|d|` for offsets. Emitted coordinates are ≤ 1e9 before
  write-back. Checks are written finite-and-bounded, so NaN and infinity fail closed. Failure:
  `RESOURCE_LIMIT` `details {limit, actual}`, no write.
- Argument ranges: `|offset| ≤ 1e6`, stroke width (0, 2e6], miter limit [1, 1000].
- Scope: every geometry command and every derivation evaluation (D-22).
- The point budget and `MaxFitOperations` (50 M elementary steps, verifier sampling included) are
  per command, aggregated, with cooperative cancellation; over-budget or cancelled commands write
  nothing. Budget injection is a Core test seam only; no CLI or MCP option raises a budget.

### Operations, operands and writes

- Commands (CLI and MCP at parity): `path bool|union|offset|stroke|transform|fit|split|inspect|
  from-node` and destructive `text outline` (`path_*`, `outline_text`).
- Shape-to-path: rectangles with circular corners of `r_eff = min(cornerRadius, width/2,
  height/2)` (what Skia draws, normative for every renderer; the SVG exporter is fixed to emit
  `rx = r_eff`, a declared defect fix), ellipses, lines, polygons, paths; frames, groups, images
  and unknown nodes are `NODE_NOT_OUTLINABLE`.
- Text to outlines: through a Core `IGlyphOutlineProvider` (Skia implementation), unhinted at
  units-per-em, strict bundled fonts by default, reporting the font and the scaler; text keeps id,
  name, order, transform and fill.
- Operands: CLI literal `d`, `@file` (inspected; refused where inspection is not admitted) or
  `<file>.agd.json#<id>` through the store-free file engine; MCP structured, confined, bounded
  operands (`{d}` or `{document, nodeId}`, operand-only files ≤ 1 MiB), never `@file`.
- Evaluation space: the write target's local space, else its parent's, else document space.
- Writes: one `ApplyPatch` with `expectedRevn` pinned to the revision read (`REVISION_CONFLICT`).
- New errors: `PATH_TRANSFORM_SINGULAR`, `NODE_NOT_OUTLINABLE`, `PATH_OPERAND_INVALID` (replaces
  `PATH_OP_FAILED` for malformed input), `PATH_TOLERANCE_UNMET`; results carry a typed code.
- Every result reports `pathData`, `algorithm`, `tolerance`, `maxDeviation`,
  `referenceTolerance`, `measuredDeviation`, `sampleSpacing`, segment and contour counts, the
  topology-event counts and list, `coincidentRuns`, `clipperStages`, `clipperCalls`,
  `arcsConverted`, and `platformSensitive: [glyphs]` when glyphs were used.

### Determinism

- Same input, options and algorithm give byte-identical output per platform and runtime. AGD
  stages use only IEEE `+ − × ÷ sqrt`, sequentially (no hash-order iteration, FMA intrinsics or
  libm); Clipper is reached only through booleans, whose code calls only rounding and min/max. An
  IL scan proves it.
- Glyph outlines are the only platform-sensitive input. Their class waits on the owner (inbox):
  the recommended answer declares `text outline` results a per-platform class, flagged
  `platformSensitive: [glyphs]` and recomputed from inputs; converted outlines are ordinary
  cross-platform paths afterwards. Everything else is expected byte-identical across platforms;
  a difference is a determinism defect, verified once the macOS lane (change 008) runs the goldens.
- **Algorithm identifier**: results report `agd-geometry/1`, and goldens are bound to it. While
  change 011 lands across several byte-changing pull requests, builds report `agd-geometry/1-dev`,
  which promises nothing and whose goldens may be regenerated; its freeze pull request switches to
  `agd-geometry/1` and freezes the goldens under an append-only digest manifest that a CI guard
  protects (any modified, renamed or deleted frozen golden needs a higher identifier in the same
  diff). Platform-sensitive goldens live in per-platform directories.

### Acceptance oracles (independent of the code under test)

1. Dense oracle: emitted boundary sampled at ≤ T/100, distances to the exact ideal to ≤ T/1000,
   asserting `oracle ≤ maxDeviation + T/50`; two-sided for the forward class (transform, fit,
   split, shape-to-path, stroke, offset) and for booleans without events, otherwise one-sided
   excluding a stated disc around each reported unrefined intersection; known-bad self-tests
   (a square-fallback offset, a seeded 0.2·T wedge crack) must be rejected.
2. Stage-planner table: every op × geometry × fill rule × offset sign × operand kind, the
   feasibility rule from exact expressions, `minFeasibleTolerance`, and a pinned `split-reunite`
   row feasible at 0.008.
3. Matcher and refinement property tests (100 % match within `q_c`; crossings at 90 to 5°).
4. Chained round trips in curves mode (`square-minus-circle`, `split-reunite`, ≥ 3 reloads, at
   0.01, at each step's minimum and at 0.008 where feasible): structure equality, full reuse,
   zero events, and an XOR oracle with no residue ≥ T.
5. A crossing-strand reproduction (stroke, round and miter clearance, difference, union) with no
   hand guard; stroke equivalence against live strokes at 4× under scale 2, 2×1 and rotation 30°;
   shape-conversion, `path offset` surface, magnitude, trig-sweep and golden checks; text versus
   render for bundled Inter at 12 to 96 (increment 5).

## Alternatives

- **Re-fit only**: round trips over stored fitted results leave slivers.
- **A certified deviation bound** (a per-chain a-priori error budget): every review round found
  new numerical edge cases without changing the mechanisms; replaced by measured and reported,
  validated by an independent oracle. Only the feasibility margin survives.
- **Vertex snapping, a snap radius of T, containment-only matching, a fixed refinement radius**:
  each consumed tolerance, merged real features, missed siblings or rejected ordinary crossings.
- **Keeping `joined` or the 2.0 miter default**: a parallel spelling of `path stroke` or two
  defaults for one join; excluded by the direct-cutover rule. Clamping source limits breaks stroke
  equivalence.
- **Constructed-vertex tables** for one-step miter clearances on stroked cutters: the two-step
  recipe exists.
- **`ClipperOffset`**, **Skia PathOps or the Skia stroker as authority**, **Clipper's Z channel**:
  libm in joins and a square fallback; native and version-dependent (test oracle only); unavailable
  in the shipped build.
- **A larger Clipper scale**: polygon output would leave the 0.001 grid.
- **Declaring rotations and arcs platform-sensitive**: contradicts the determinism rule.

## Revisit when

The owner answers the glyph platform class with another option (a managed TrueType reader, or
glyph differences as a qualification defect); the matcher or refinement property tests find an
in-contract miss; macOS goldens diverge in a non-glyph class; consumers need host-independent text
outlines or a proved deviation bound; a curve-native boolean engine becomes qualifiable.
